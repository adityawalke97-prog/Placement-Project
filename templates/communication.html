GUIDE 01

The 7 Cs of effective communication

Use these seven principles to make your message easy to understand and act on.

1. Clear

Have one main purpose. Avoid confusing or vague wording.

Unclear: “Do that thing soon.” Clear: “Please send the report by 4 PM today.”
2. Concise

Use only the words needed. Remove repeated ideas and unnecessary details.

3. Concrete

Use facts, examples, dates and specific details instead of general statements.

4. Correct

Check grammar, names, numbers, facts and the recipient before sending a message.

5. Coherent

Arrange ideas logically so each sentence connects to the next.

6. Complete

Include the information the listener needs: what, why, when and next step.

7. Courteous

Use respectful language, even when correcting a mistake or disagreeing.

GUIDE 02

How the communication process works

Communication is a two-way process. A message is sent, understood and responded to.

  1. Sender: the person who has information or an idea.
  2. Encoding: converting the idea into words, signs or gestures.
  3. Message: the information being shared.
  4. Channel: the method, such as face-to-face, phone, email or chat.
  5. Receiver: the person who gets the message.
  6. Decoding: interpreting what the message means.
  7. Feedback: the receiver's response that shows whether the message was understood.
Example: A team leader asks you to finish a task by Friday (message). You repeat the deadline to confirm (feedback). This prevents misunderstanding.

What is communication noise?

Noise is anything that interferes with a message. It can be background sound, poor internet, unclear wording, stress, assumptions or unfamiliar terminology.

GUIDE 03

Types of communication

Choose the communication style that suits the situation and the message.

Verbal / oral

Spoken communication in meetings, calls, conversations and presentations. Focus on clarity, pace and tone.

Non-verbal

Body posture, facial expressions, gestures, eye contact and personal space. Make sure these support your words.

Written

Emails, reports, notices and chat messages. Organise the message and check it before sending.

Visual

Charts, diagrams, slides, symbols and images. Keep visuals simple and label them clearly.

Choosing the right channel

Use a quick chat for a simple update, a call for a discussion that needs immediate clarification, and email when a formal record or detailed information is needed.

GUIDE 04

Barriers to communication

Recognise what blocks understanding and learn how to reduce it.

Language barrier

Unfamiliar words, fast speech or complex sentences. Use simple words, explain terms and ask for clarification.

Physical barrier

Noise, distance or poor connectivity. Choose a quieter place and confirm important details in writing.

Emotional barrier

Stress, anger or fear can affect how a message is understood. Pause, calm down and speak respectfully.

Cultural barrier

People may have different communication styles. Avoid assumptions and ask polite questions when unsure.

Listening barrier

Interrupting or multitasking causes missed information. Give attention and summarise what you heard.

Useful phrase: “I want to make sure I understood correctly. Could you please explain that part again?”
GUIDE 05

Organise your thoughts before speaking

Use simple structures so your answer has a beginning, middle and end.

PREP method for opinions

P — PointState your main opinion.
R — ReasonExplain why you think so.
E — ExampleGive a real or relevant example.
P — PointRestate your key message briefly.
Example topic: Teamwork
“I believe teamwork is important. It combines different skills and ideas. For example, in a college project, one person can handle the backend while another works on the interface. That's why teamwork can improve a project's outcome.”

When explaining a process

Use: First → Next → After that → Finally. This makes instructions and project explanations easier to follow.

GUIDE 06

Improve speaking clarity and fluency

Being understood is more important than copying a particular accent.

Speak at a comfortable pace

Slow down slightly on important points. Very fast speech can make words difficult to understand.

Use pauses

Pause between ideas instead of filling every silence with “umm”, “actually” or “like”.

Practise word stress

Listen to how a word is pronounced and which syllable is stressed. Use a dictionary with audio to check unfamiliar words.

Read aloud

Read a short paragraph for five minutes. Focus on complete sounds, natural pauses and clear endings of words.

Record and review

Record a short practice explanation. Check whether the message is clear and whether you speak too quickly.

Do not try to remove your natural accent. Work on understandable pronunciation, clear words and confident delivery.
GUIDE 07

Write professional emails

A professional email should make its purpose clear and tell the reader what action is needed.

Basic email structure

Subject: Request for project meeting

Hello [Name],

I would like to discuss the current project progress and clarify the next steps. Could we schedule a short meeting this week?

Thank you for your time.

Regards,
[Your Name]

Email checklist

  • Write a short, specific subject line.
  • Use an appropriate greeting.
  • Explain the purpose in the first few lines.
  • Use short paragraphs and a polite tone.
  • State the action or response you need.
  • Check the recipient, attachment and spelling before sending.
Avoid: ALL CAPS, unclear subjects such as “Hi”, slang in formal emails and sending a message without checking attachments.
GUIDE 08

Chat and messaging etiquette

Short messages still need context, clarity and respect.

Give context

Instead of “Send it”, write “Could you share the updated Java assignment file?”

Ask one clear question at a time

Break complicated requests into readable points.

Respect availability

Do not expect an immediate reply unless the matter is urgent and agreed.

Use emojis carefully

They can make casual messages friendly, but avoid them in formal or sensitive communication.

Confirm important decisions

Summarise deadlines, responsibilities and decisions in the project group.

Better message: “Hi team, I have completed the login page. I will work on database connectivity next and share an update by tomorrow afternoon.”
GUIDE 09

Give a clear presentation

Help your audience understand one main message instead of reading slides word for word.

Simple presentation structure

OpeningGreet the audience, introduce the topic and explain why it matters.
Main pointsPresent two to four key ideas in a logical order, with examples.
ConclusionSummarise the message and invite questions.

Useful opening lines

“Good morning everyone. Today, I am going to explain our project, the problem it solves and how we developed it.”

Presentation tips

  • Practise aloud instead of memorising every word.
  • Keep slides readable and avoid large paragraphs.
  • Face the audience and speak at a steady pace.
  • Use examples to explain technical ideas.
  • If you lose your place, pause and continue from the next point.
GUIDE 10

Handle disagreement and feedback

Professional communication means discussing problems without attacking people.

When you disagree

  • Listen to the complete point.
  • Acknowledge what makes sense.
  • Explain your concern with a reason or fact.
  • Suggest another option.
“I understand why you suggested this design. My concern is that it may be difficult to use on mobile. Could we try a simpler layout?”

When receiving feedback

Listen without interrupting. Ask for an example if the feedback is unclear. Thank the person and decide what improvement you can make.

“Thank you for the feedback. Could you show me which part needs improvement so I can correct it?”

When giving feedback

Be specific, respectful and focused on the work. Mention the issue, its effect and a possible improvement.

GUIDE 11

Speak confidently on phone calls

Without body language, your words, tone and listening become especially important.

  1. Greet the person and introduce yourself.
  2. Check whether it is a convenient time to talk.
  3. Explain the purpose of your call briefly.
  4. Listen carefully and take notes if details matter.
  5. Repeat important names, dates or next steps.
  6. End politely and thank the person.
“Hello, this is [Your Name]. I am calling regarding the internship application. Is this a good time to speak?”

If you cannot hear clearly

Say: “I'm sorry, the connection is not clear. Could you please repeat that?” Do not pretend you heard important information.

GUIDE 12

A 30-day communication improvement plan

Build one habit at a time. Consistency is more useful than practising for hours once a week.

Week 1 — Build the base

Read aloud daily, learn five useful phrases, introduce yourself and describe your routine in simple English.

Week 2 — Improve conversations

Practise greetings, open questions, follow-up questions, active listening and polite clarification.

Week 3 — Professional communication

Write one sample email, give a project update, explain a technical concept simply and practise respectful disagreement.

Week 4 — Apply your skills

Give a short presentation, explain a project, participate in a group discussion and review your speaking recording.

At the end of each week, write down: What improved? What still feels difficult? What one habit will I continue next week?
